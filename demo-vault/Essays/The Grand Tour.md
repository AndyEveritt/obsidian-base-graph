In the late 1970s the outer planets lined up in a way that happens about once every 175 years. A single spacecraft could swing past [[Jupiter]], [[Saturn]], [[Uranus]] and [[Neptune]], using a [[Gravity assist]] at each one to reach the next. [[NASA]] sent two: [[Voyager 1]] and [[Voyager 2]].=
## Two routes

[[Voyager 2]] launched first, on 20 August 1977, on the slower route that kept the whole tour open. [[Voyager 1]] followed on 5 September on a faster path, and overtook [[Voyager 2]] within a few months.

At [[Saturn]], [[Voyager 1]] was steered close to [[Titan]] to study its thick atmosphere. That flyby bent its path up out of the plane of the planets, so the rest of the tour fell to [[Voyager 2]].

## One spacecraft, four planets

Each [[Gravity assist]] took a little of a planet's orbital speed. [[Voyager 2]] reached [[Uranus]] in 1986 and [[Neptune]] in 1989, and it is still the only spacecraft to have visited either. At [[Neptune]] it found geysers on [[Triton]].

## Leaving

Both probes carry the [[Golden Record]]. In 1990 [[Voyager 1]] turned back to take the [[Pale Blue Dot]] at [[Carl Sagan]]'s request, then crossed the edge of the [[Heliosphere]] in 2012. [[Voyager 2]] followed it into interstellar space in 2018, still sending data home through the same antennas that tracked it past [[Jupiter]].
