Liquid water was once thought to need a planet close to its star. Then the outer moons started giving it away, and the search for [[Ocean worlds]] became one of the main reasons to fly to the outer planets.

## Europa

[[Galileo]] orbited [[Jupiter]] from 1995 to 2003. Its magnetometer found that [[Europa]] disturbs Jupiter's magnetic field the way a salty, conducting ocean would. [[Galileo]] saw the same hint at [[Ganymede]]. At the end of the mission it was steered into [[Jupiter]] so it could never crash onto [[Europa]] and contaminate it.

## Enceladus

[[Cassini-Huygens]] reached [[Saturn]] in 2004 and found plumes of water ice spraying from the south pole of [[Enceladus]] a year later. It flew through them again and again, tasting salt, silica and hydrogen from an ocean under the ice. [[Enceladus]] had gone from a small, bright moon to one of the most promising places to look for life.

[[Cassini-Huygens]] also found signs of an ocean under [[Titan]]'s crust. Like [[Galileo]], it ended by diving into its planet in 2017, to keep [[Enceladus]] and [[Titan]] clean.

## Going back

Two missions are now on their way to [[Jupiter]]'s moons. [[JUICE]] launched in 2023 and will end up in orbit around [[Ganymede]], after flying past [[Europa]] twice. [[Europa Clipper]] launched in 2024 and will make dozens of close flybys of [[Europa]] from 2030.

Between them, [[JUICE]] and [[Europa Clipper]] will test whether [[Europa]] has what life needs. If it does, [[Enceladus]] will be next, and the list of [[Ocean worlds]] will keep growing. [[Europa Clipper]] is the first step.
